# Chapter 1: Me

Who am I?

This seems like an important question to answer before everything else. Everything I write in this book comes from a specific person — me — so you should probably know who that is. I wrote this book because I thought it might be fun to read it again in a few decades and see how much I've changed, or haven't changed. We'll see. Also it's cool to write a book because I can quote myself. Or is that lame? It's cool when people bring out verses from the Bible and quote stuff. Speaking of the Bible, it's gonna be mentioned a lot because I have some problems with it, but I'll talk about that later.

I'm a real person who lives on Earth. Am I sure? No, but what's the point of doubting whether I'm real or not? If I'm real then I'm right, and if I'm not then it doesn't matter anyway.

Wow. That was a long detour from the sentence I wanted to start the chapter with, but here it comes.

---

## The person in my head vs. the person in the room

There's a version of me that only exists when I'm alone and thinking. In that version I'm articulate. I'm calm. I have the perfect response to every argument. I know exactly what I believe and why I believe it.

Then someone actually talks to me and half of that disappears.

Some of it comes back when I'm writing, which is probably why I'm writing this book instead of standing on a street corner yelling my opinions at strangers. Although depending on the day, I might be closer to the street corner version than I'd like to admit.

What you're reading is filtered — not in a fake way. I'm not trying to trick you into thinking I'm cooler or more put-together than I am. Writing just gives me time. It lets me finish a thought before something else interrupts it. It's the closest thing I have to being the person I think I could be if my brain would cooperate for five consecutive minutes.

A psychologist tested me a few years ago and said I'm apparently a very smart person. Processing, reasoning, whatever categories they measure — all high. But my working memory was only average, which relative to everything else is basically bad. Smart with a bad working memory is like having a powerful engine and a tiny gas tank. You can go fast, but not for long.

I'll walk into a room and forget why. I'll think of something brilliant in the shower and lose it by the time I dry off. I'll have a conversation with someone and forget a detail they told me two minutes ago, then pretend I remember because I don't want them to feel unimportant. They are important. I just can't hold the information.

Sometimes people think I'm not listening when I'm actually listening very hard and failing to retain. Sometimes I seem careless when I'm overwhelmed. I write things down not because I'm organized but because I know I'll forget.

And then sometimes — the topic is right, the focus is there, the impulse is quiet — and I'm sharp in a way that surprises even me. I connect two ideas nobody else connected. I say the right thing at the right time. Those moments are addictive. They make me believe the potential is real, not just a number on a test score from years ago.

I love that feeling. Knowing things. Being right. Having an answer when someone asks. Is that ego? Probably. But there's a difference between thinking you're better than everyone and just enjoying your own brain working. I try to stay in the second one. I don't always succeed.

---

## What I love

I love having fun. Sports, even though I'm not the best. Music — good music makes me so happy. Love itself. It feels great. I love when people make me laugh, and I love making people laugh. If I can make you laugh, I feel like I've said something real, even when I couldn't say it straight.

I love the feeling of understanding something for the first time. Not memorizing it. Not being told it. Actually *getting* it — that click when a foggy concept suddenly has edges. I chase that feeling constantly. It's why I fall down rabbit holes at two in the morning about things that have nothing to do with my life.

I love competition, even when I lose. The tension before something happens — right before a game starts, before a song drops, before a joke lands. I love sincerity. Inside jokes that only make sense to two people. Being trusted.

I love food. Not just eating it — every culture on Earth figured out different ways to make dead plants and animals taste incredible. I love a meal someone made because they cared about you. I love a meal so good it makes you stop talking.

I love finishing something I started, even if it took three weeks, twelve false starts, and one night staring at a screen eating cereal. I love the version of me that follows through. I don't meet him as often as I'd like.

Technically there's an infinite number of things I love, since I can plug any high number into "food that is X/10" and it works. 9.03839. 8.99954. You get the idea.

---

## What I hate

Bad music. I can't define it in a way that would hold up in court, but I know it instantly — something in the sound that feels like the artist didn't care, or like they're trying too hard and the effort shows.

Loud noises. I have sensitive ears and get triggered easily. Fireworks are a personal attack. Sudden bangs make my whole body flinch before my brain catches up. People say "just get used to it." You can learn to tolerate a punch in the shoulder too. That doesn't make it stop hurting.

Annoying voices. I know this makes me sound terrible. I can't explain it. Some voices just grate — not accents, not speech impediments, just a quality that makes every sentence feel too long. I hate that I hate it. It happens, then I feel guilty, then annoyed, then guilty about being annoyed.

Obnoxious people. Lame people — subjective, I know, and I've definitely been lame without realizing it. But some people drain the air out of a room, and I notice.

What bothers me most is people who lack common sense. Not ignorance — that can be fixed. Not disagreement — that's healthy. But the refusal to think. Shutting out any idea that doesn't match what they were taught. Treating a new perspective like a personal insult. Choosing comfort over being correct. I catch myself doing versions of this too, rejecting an idea because of who said it instead of what they said. But I try to notice. Some people don't. Some are proud of not trying.

That one drives me insane.

## The brain that won't sit still

I'm impulsive. I pursue short-term happiness even though I know hard work pays off and living this way is bad. I've always been this way. It almost feels like a curse — not dramatic, just the quiet daily kind where you watch yourself choose the wrong thing, see yourself choosing it, and choose it anyway. Like being a passenger in your own body.

Eat better. Sleep earlier. Finish the project before starting a new one. Save money. Have the hard conversation. The knowledge is always there. It's like having a GPS that works perfectly while I drive straight into a lake.

I have ADHD. Perhaps some kind of autism. I don't say that for sympathy — explaining is different from excusing. I still live with the consequences. But it helps me understand why the lock is hard to pick.

When I like something, I get addicted fast. Not always clinically — but something catches my attention and suddenly it's all I want to do, talk about, and think about. I dive in completely. Then eventually the fire moves. Music phases. Game phases. Topic phases. Person phases, sometimes, though that's the hardest one and I'm not unpacking it here.

People call it hyperfocus when it's convenient and a problem when it's inconvenient. When I'm in it, I'm *in* it. When I'm out, I can barely remember caring that much. It makes me wonder how much of my personality is actually personality and how much is just whatever my brain latched onto this month.

I used to be afraid of everything — not useful fear, the kind that shrinks your world before you even know if something's dangerous. I lived there a long time. Smaller. Safer. Lonely in a way I didn't have words for.

Now I'm living too recklessly. I don't know when the swing happened. Maybe I traded one problem for another. Maybe it's the other side of the same coin. The voice that said "don't" got quieter. The voice that said "why not" got louder. Speed without steering.

Maybe the truth is somewhere in the middle. Maybe that's what the next few decades are for.

---

## Ego, guilt, and the rest

I value myself a lot. I think my thoughts are worth writing in a book I might read decades from now. I think I'm funny sometimes. I think I'm right, often — too often.

Sometimes that makes people think I'm selfish. Sometimes they're right. I get wrapped up in what I want and forget to check if the person in front of me is okay. I don't mean to. Impulse doesn't ask permission, and the part of my brain that says "consider others first" sometimes loses the race to the part that says "this would feel good right now."

I hate that about myself. Not writing it to excuse it — writing it because pretending I'm always considerate would be its own kind of lie.

There's a difference between valuing yourself and devaluing others. I hope I mostly live on the right side of that line. Ask the people who've known me longest. Their answer is probably more accurate than mine.

---

## Why bother with all this

I'm not a fixed object. I can love a song and hear it in a commercial and hate it forever. I can feel one way on Tuesday and another on Wednesday. This book is going to be inconsistent. I hope that's okay.

If you came here looking for a neat box labeled "who this author is," you're in the wrong chapter. Maybe the wrong book. I'm more like a browser with too many tabs open, and half of them are playing sound.

I started here because everything else in this book — the universe, morality, God, the bored restless version of me in later chapters — all of it comes through this filter. If you don't know me, you might read a sentence and think I'm arrogant, or cold, or reckless. You might be right, depending on the day.

Don't believe everything you're told. Including me. Especially me.

Honestly, it's too complicated to say everything about who I am in one chapter. You'll see more of me as you keep reading — the philosophical me, the angry me, the me who can't always live up to the standards he sets for other people.

But for now: I love love and hate loud noises. I score high on tests and forget why I walked into the room. I know better and do worse anyway. I used to be afraid of everything and now might be moving too fast.

I wrote a book so I could quote myself. Still not sure if that's cool or lame.

We'll see.
