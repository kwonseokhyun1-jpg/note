# Chapter 1: Me

Who am I?

This seems like an important question to answer before everything else. This section exists to provide you context. Everything I write is based on who I am as a person, so I thought it might be important to give an idea of who is writing from this perspective.

I wrote this book because I thought it might be fun to read it again in a few decades and see how much I've changed, or haven't changed — we'll see. Also it's cool to write a book because I can quote myself. Or is that lame? It's cool when people bring out verses from the Bible and quote stuff. Speaking of the Bible, it's kind of gonna be mentioned a lot because I have some problems with it, but I'll talk about that later.

So who am I?

I'm a real person who lives on Earth. Am I sure? No, but what's the point of doubting whether I'm real or not? If I'm real then I'm right, and if I'm not then it doesn't matter anyway.

Wow. That was a long detour from the sentence I wanted to start the chapter with, but here it comes.

---

There's a lot of things I love and a lot of things I hate.

I love having fun, just like most people. I love many different sports, even though I'm not the best. I love when people make me laugh, and I love making people laugh. I love love — it feels great. I love music. Listening to good music makes me so happy.

I hate bad music. And loud noises. I have sensitive ears, and I get triggered easily by loud sounds. I hate when people have annoying voices. Obviously that's subjective, but I can't handle when people have frustrating voices and I can't explain it. I hate obnoxious people — it's too much. I hate lame people. Again, subjective. I really hate people who lack common sense. People with no critical thinking skills who automatically shut out any ideas that don't align with what they were taught.

Technically, there's an infinite number for both, since I can insert any number in an applicable situation and it would work. Like, I love eating food that is X/10 where X is any number that's relatively high. It could be like 9.03839 or 8.99954 — like it could be infinite.

While I was first thinking about what to write here, I had no idea I was gonna be explaining stuff this much, and I probably forgot most things I originally thought about. I'm a forgetful person. My short-term/working memory is bad. According to some test I did with a psychologist a few years ago. Well, it said it was average, but relative to my other skills it's bad. Because I'm apparently a very smart person.

I really am pretty smart sometimes, and I love feeling smart. Hey, that's something else I love. Do I have a big ego? Well, I think I value myself a lot. Sometimes it could cause others to think I'm selfish. Sometimes I can't help it.

---

I'm an impulsive person and I make a lot of mistakes. I pursue short-term happiness and temporary pleasure even though I know hard work pays off and it's bad to live this way. I've always been this way, and it almost feels like a curse.

I have ADHD. Perhaps I have some kind of autism. I used to be afraid of everything; now I'm living too recklessly. When I like something I tend to get addicted very fast, and the addiction lasts a long time before randomly changing to something else.

Honestly, it's too complicated to just say everything about who I am all in this chapter. In other chapters you'll see more of me.

But since I'm already here, and since I apparently can't stop once I start, let me try anyway.

---

## The version of me that exists in my head

There's a version of me that only exists when I'm alone and thinking. In that version I'm articulate. I'm calm. I have the perfect response to every argument. I know exactly what I believe and why I believe it, and I can explain it in a way that makes other people nod and say, "Huh, I never thought about it like that."

Then someone actually talks to me and half of that disappears.

Not all of it. Some of it comes back when I'm writing, which is probably why I'm writing this book instead of just standing on a street corner and yelling my opinions at strangers. Although honestly, depending on the day, I might be closer to the street corner version than I'd like to admit.

The point is: what you're reading is filtered. Not in a fake way — I mean I'm not trying to trick you into thinking I'm cooler or smarter or more put-together than I am. But writing gives me time. Writing lets me finish a thought before something else interrupts it. Writing is the closest thing I have to being the person I think I could be if my brain would just cooperate for five consecutive minutes.

So when I say "I'm a smart person," I mean it, but I also mean it with an asterisk the size of a billboard. I'm smart in bursts. I'm smart when the topic grabs me. I'm smart when I'm not bored, which is a smaller window than you'd think. I'm smart when I'm not impulsive, which is an even smaller window.

And then sometimes I'm just smart enough to know I'm being dumb, which is its own special kind of torture.

---

## Things I love (the incomplete, ever-expanding list)

I already said I love fun, sports, laughter, love, and music. But if I'm going to write a whole chapter about me, I should probably go a little deeper than a list that sounds like it belongs on a dating profile written by someone who gave up halfway through.

I love the feeling of understanding something for the first time. Not memorizing it. Not being told it. Actually *getting* it — that little click in your brain when a concept that was foggy suddenly has edges. I chase that feeling constantly. It's probably why I read about things I don't need to know, watch videos about topics I'll never use, and fall down rabbit holes at two in the morning about subjects that have nothing to do with my life.

I love competition, even when I lose. Especially when I lose, sometimes, because losing makes winning feel real later. I love the tension before something happens — the moment right before a game starts, before a song drops, before someone tells a joke and you can feel the setup landing. I love sincerity. I love when people are genuinely themselves without performing. I love inside jokes that only make sense to two people. I love being trusted.

I love food, obviously. Not just eating it — I love the idea that every culture on Earth figured out different ways to make dead plants and animals taste incredible. That's beautiful. That's human. I love a meal that someone made because they cared about you, even if it's not that good. I love a meal that's so good it makes you stop talking. Both are rare in their own way.

I love feeling capable. I love finishing something I started, even if it took me three weeks and twelve false starts and one night where I did nothing but stare at the screen and eat cereal. I love the version of me that follows through, because I don't meet him as often as I'd like.

And I love feeling smart. I said that already. I know. But it's worth repeating because it's honest, and this chapter is supposed to be honest. I like knowing things. I like being right. I like when someone asks me a question and I actually have an answer. Is that ego? Yeah, probably. But it's also joy. There's a difference between thinking you're better than everyone and just enjoying the feeling of your own brain working. I try to stay in the second one. I don't always succeed.

## Things I hate (also incomplete, also subjective, also infinite)

I hate bad music. I already said that too. But what is bad music? I can't define it in a way that would hold up in court, and yet I know it the instant I hear it. It's not about genre. It's about something in the sound that feels wrong — like the artist didn't care, or like they're trying too hard to make me feel something and the effort is visible. Bad music feels like someone lying to my ears.

I hate loud noises. I have sensitive ears and I get triggered easily by loud sounds. Fireworks are a personal attack. Sudden bangs make my whole body flinch before my brain catches up. People say "just get used to it" and I want to ask them if they've ever tried getting used to a punch in the shoulder. You can learn to tolerate it. That doesn't mean it stops being awful.

I hate annoying voices. I know this makes me sound like a terrible person. I can't explain it. Some voices just grate on me — not accents, not speech impediments, nothing I'm proud of being bothered by — just a quality in certain people's voices that makes every sentence feel like it's taking too long. I hate that I hate it. I don't choose it. It happens and then I feel guilty and then I feel annoyed and then I feel guilty about being annoyed. It's a whole cycle.

I hate obnoxious people. People who take up all the space in a room and think volume equals personality. I hate lame people — and yes, I know "lame" is subjective, and yes, I know everyone is lame to someone, and yes, I have definitely been lame to other people without realizing it. I'm not claiming immunity. I'm just saying: some people drain the air out of a room, and I notice.

But the thing I hate most — the thing that actually bothers me on a deeper level than bad music or loud noises or frustrating voices — is people who lack common sense. People with no critical thinking skills who automatically shut out any ideas that don't align with what they were taught. People who hear a new perspective and treat it like a personal insult. People who would rather be comfortable than correct.

That one gets to me because it feels like a waste. Like having a brain and choosing not to use it. And I know that's harsh. I know everyone has reasons. I know upbringing and fear and community and identity all play a part. I'm not saying I'm immune to it either — I catch myself doing it sometimes, rejecting an idea because of who said it instead of what they said. But I try to notice when I'm doing it. Some people don't try. Some people are proud of not trying.

And that drives me insane.

## The infinite list problem

I said earlier that there's technically an infinite number of things I love and hate, because I can plug any high number into "food that is X/10" and it works. 9.03839. 8.99954. 9.999999 recurring.

I think about stuff like that more than a normal person probably should.

It's the same with opinions. I can love something in one context and hate it in another. I can love a song and then hear it in a commercial and hate it forever. I can hate a person on Tuesday and then learn one thing about them on Wednesday and feel conflicted for a week. Humans are inconsistent. I'm inconsistent. This book is going to be inconsistent. I hope that's okay.

If you came here looking for a clean, organized summary of a stable personality — a neat little box labeled "who this author is" — you're in the wrong chapter. Maybe the wrong book. I'm not a fixed object. I'm more like a browser with too many tabs open, and half of them are playing sound.

## Memory, intelligence, and the gap between them

I'm forgetful. I said that already as well. I'm saying it again because it's one of the most defining things about me and also one of the most frustrating.

I'll walk into a room and forget why. I'll think of something brilliant in the shower and lose it by the time I dry off. I'll have a conversation with someone and forget a detail they told me two minutes ago, and then I have to pretend I remember because I don't want to make them feel unimportant. They are important. I just can't hold the information. It's not a choice.

The psychologist test said my working memory was average. Average. But relative to my other cognitive scores, it was a weak point. Everything else was higher. Processing, reasoning, whatever categories they measure — apparently I'm "very smart." But smart with a bad working memory is like having a powerful engine and a tiny gas tank. You can go fast, but not for long, and you spend a lot of time refueling.

Sometimes I wonder if people think I'm not listening when I'm actually listening very hard and just failing to retain. Sometimes I wonder if I seem careless when I'm actually overwhelmed. Sometimes I write things down not because I'm organized but because I know I'll forget, and forgetting feels like losing a piece of myself I wasn't done using yet.

And then sometimes — sometimes — everything aligns. The topic is right, the focus is there, the impulse is quiet, and I'm sharp in a way that surprises even me. I solve something fast. I connect two ideas nobody else connected. I say the right thing at the right time. Those moments are addictive. They make me believe the potential is real, not just a test score on a piece of paper from a few years ago.

I love feeling smart. There's that line again. I can't help it. It's one of the best feelings I know.

---

## Ego, selfishness, and the mirror I don't always want to look into

Do I have a big ego?

I think I value myself a lot. That's the honest answer. I think my thoughts matter. I think my perspective is worth writing down in a book that I might read decades from now. I think I'm funny, sometimes. I think I'm right, often — too often, probably.

Sometimes that causes other people to think I'm selfish. Sometimes they're right.

I can get so wrapped up in what I want, what I feel, what I think, that I forget to check whether the person in front of me is okay. I don't mean to. I don't wake up and decide to be selfish. But impulse doesn't ask permission, and ADHD doesn't send a calendar invite before it derails your afternoon, and the part of my brain that says "consider others first" sometimes loses the race to the part that says "this would feel good right now."

I hate that about myself. I don't write about it to excuse it. I write about it because it's true, and this chapter is supposed to be true, and pretending I'm always considerate would be another kind of lie — the kind that looks noble from the outside and rots from the inside.

I also think there's a difference between valuing yourself and devaluing others. I hope I mostly live on the right side of that line. I don't know for sure. Ask the people who've known me longest. Their answer is probably more accurate than mine.

## Impulse, pleasure, and the curse of knowing better

I'm impulsive. I make a lot of mistakes. I pursue short-term happiness and temporary pleasure even though I know hard work pays off and it's bad to live this way.

I've always been this way. It almost feels like a curse — not in a dramatic, literary sense, but in the quiet, daily sense where you watch yourself choose the wrong thing and you can see yourself choosing it and you still choose it, like you're a passenger in your own body.

I know what I should do. Eat better. Sleep earlier. Finish the project before starting a new one. Save money. Practice the skill instead of scrolling. Have the difficult conversation instead of avoiding it. The knowledge is there. It's always there. It's like having a GPS that works perfectly while I drive straight into a lake.

And the worst part — the part that makes it feel cursed — is that the pleasure is real. The short-term happiness is real. The dopamine is real. My brain rewards me immediately for bad decisions and makes me wait weeks for good ones. That's not a character flaw I invented. That's wiring. That's chemistry. That's whatever combination of genetics and environment and choices piled up over years into the person typing this sentence.

I have ADHD. Perhaps I have some kind of autism. I don't say that for sympathy. I say it because it explains some things, and explaining is different from excusing. I still have to live with the consequences of my choices. I still have to try. But understanding why the lock is hard to pick doesn't mean the door isn't still locked.

---

## Fear, recklessness, and the swing

I used to be afraid of everything.

I don't mean normal fear — not the useful kind that keeps you from touching fire or walking into traffic. I mean the kind that shrinks your world. The kind that makes you avoid things before you even know if they're dangerous. The kind that sits in your chest and whispers that everything is too much, too risky, too likely to go wrong.

I lived there for a long time. I know what that version of me felt like. Smaller. Safer. Lonely in a way I didn't have words for.

And now?

Now I'm living too recklessly.

I don't know exactly when the swing happened. I don't know if I traded one problem for another or if this is just the other side of the same coin. But somewhere along the way, the voice that said "don't" got quieter, and the voice that said "why not" got louder, and now I have to deal with a different set of consequences. Not fear. Not paralysis. More like speed without steering.

Maybe the truth is somewhere in the middle and I just haven't found it yet. Maybe that's what the next few decades are for — assuming I read this book again and haven't driven straight into another lake.

---

## Addiction, obsession, and the thing I'm into this month

When I like something, I tend to get addicted very fast.

Not always in the clinical sense — I'm not claiming every hobby is a disorder. But the pattern is real: something catches my attention, and suddenly it's all I want to do, all I want to talk about, all I want to think about. I dive in completely. I learn everything. I consume it at a pace that would embarrass me if anyone kept a chart.

And then, eventually, it fades. Not always because I stopped liking it. Sometimes I still like it. But the fire changes. The obsession moves. Something else becomes the new center of gravity, and the old thing drifts to the background, still liked but no longer urgent.

Music phases. Game phases. Topic phases. Person phases, sometimes, though that's the most complicated one and I don't have the courage to unpack it fully in Chapter 1.

People call it ADHD hyperfocus when it's convenient and call it a problem when it's inconvenient. All I know is: when I'm in it, I'm *in* it. And when I'm out, I can barely remember what it felt like to care that much. That whiplash is disorienting. It makes me wonder how much of my personality is actually personality and how much is just whatever my brain decided to latch onto this month.

---

## Why this chapter exists (and why it can't be enough)

I started this chapter because you should know who's talking to you.

Everything in this book — the opinions about the universe, the arguments about morality, the problems with God, the bored restless version of me in Chapter 5 and whatever comes after — all of it comes through this filter. This brain. This history. These loves and hates and fears and impulses and memories that leak out of my head faster than I can catch them.

If you don't know me, you might read a sentence and think I'm arrogant. Or cold. Or reckless. Or too emotional. Or not emotional enough. And you might be right, depending on the day and the page.

But if you know a little of where I'm coming from — the sensitive ears and the loud noises, the smart bursts and the bad memory, the ego and the guilt, the fear that became recklessness, the addictions that shift like weather — then maybe you'll read the rest with the right kind of skepticism. Not skepticism toward me, necessarily. Skepticism in general. The kind I wish more people practiced.

Don't believe everything you're told. Including me. Especially me, maybe, since I already told you I'm impulsive and forgetful and prone to tangents.

## A few more things before I stop

I love when people make me laugh. I love making people laugh. I said that at the beginning but it deserves its own moment because humor might be the most honest thing about me. If I can make you laugh, I feel like I've communicated something real, even if I couldn't say it straight.

I hate when people lack common sense. I said that too. I'll probably keep saying it in different chapters because it genuinely might be my biggest pet peeve. Not ignorance — ignorance can be fixed. Not disagreement — disagreement is healthy. But the refusal to think. The automatic rejection. The comfort of certainty in a universe that offers almost none of it.

I'm a real person who lives on Earth. I think. I'm not sure. It doesn't matter.

I wrote this book for future me, mostly. But you're here now, so — welcome. I'm not going to pretend I have everything figured out. I don't. This chapter was supposed to introduce me, and instead it became a confession, a list, a tangent, a psychology report, and a warning label all at once.

That tracks.

Honestly, it's too complicated to just say everything about who I am all in this chapter. In other chapters you'll see more of me — the bored me, the philosophical me, the angry me, the me who asks questions about God and morality and the universe and can't always live up to the standards I set for other people.

But for now, this is enough of a start.

I'm the person who loves love and hates loud noises. Who scores high on tests and forgets why he walked into the room. Who knows better and does worse anyway. Who used to be afraid of everything and now might be moving too fast in the other direction.

Who wrote a book so he could quote himself.

And who still isn't sure if that's cool or lame.

We'll see.
